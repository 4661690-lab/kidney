At 9:15 each morning, the spa belongs to a single guest.

The sauna is hot. The bath is filled with fresh water and packed with ice — poured that morning, for one person only. No one has been in that water before you.

Before the cold, you are taught how to breathe. During it, you are not alone — you are counted through, second by second. And after it comes the part almost nobody expects: fifteen minutes of guided meditation, in the clearest state your mind will reach all year.

You will be finished before ten. The rest of the day will not feel like a holiday day — it will feel sharper than that.

No experience needed. No fitness needed. One minute of courage, once.

The hour

What happens, minute by minute

9:15

Tea, and a short conversation

We sit down. A few questions about your health, your sleep, and what your body needs this morning. Nothing about the next hour will be a surprise to you.

9:20

Breath

Ten minutes, seated. Not a warm-up — this is the tool you will use in the water, and the reason the cold becomes something you can steer rather than something that happens to you.

9:30

Sauna

Twelve minutes of dry heat. Your body opens, softens, and stops holding whatever it arrived holding.

9:42

The Ice

Sixty to ninety seconds. Fresh water, fresh ice, prepared that morning for you alone. You are not left to manage it — the seconds are counted out loud, and you decide when it ends.

9:45

Stillness — guided meditation

Fifteen minutes. The part guests remember.

10:00

Tea, and one practice to take home

A single thing to keep doing after you leave — thirty seconds a day, and it is yours for good.

Why the meditation is the point

Cold water lifts noradrenaline several times over and holds it there for an hour or more. What that produces is a rare thing: complete calm and complete alertness at the same time. Most people never reach it on purpose, and almost nobody knows what to do with it when they do.

You are not left alone with it. This part is led — drawn from qigong and meditation practice, shaped around what your body just did. The ice is the doorway. This is the room.

Questions

Is it safe?+
Cold water immersion is safe for healthy adults, and you will be supervised the entire time — never left alone at any point. Tell us beforehand about heart conditions, high blood pressure, epilepsy or pregnancy, and we will adapt or advise against it honestly. Sessions are not available after alcohol.
Does it hurt?+
No. For the first ten to fifteen seconds your body will want to gasp — that happens to everyone, and it passes. The rest is intense but calm. You are taught exactly how to breathe through it before you get in, and the seconds are counted out loud so you never have to wonder how long is left.
What if I can't stay in for a minute?+
Then you get out. Thirty seconds is a complete session. Nothing about this is a test, and there is no one to prove anything to.
Do I need to be fit?+
No. This has nothing to do with fitness. Guests in their sixties do this. It asks for one minute of willingness, not conditioning.
Can two of us come together?+
The bath takes one person, so the ritual is designed for one. Two guests can book two consecutive mornings, or ask about a private session for a couple.
How cold is the water?+
Between 8 and 12°C — measured, not guessed, and adjusted to you. Cold enough to work. Not so cold that you lose control of your breathing.
What should I bring?+
Nothing. Robe, towels, slippers and water are provided. Swimwear is worn in the sauna and the bath.
Is there anything longer?+
Yes — a ninety-minute session with three descents, each colder than the last. Ask about it on the morning.
